What We Audit

Six Fronts, One Report

The audit covers the places small businesses actually get hit — not a checkbox exercise.

🌐

External Attack Surface

What an attacker sees from the outside: exposed services, open ports, DNS posture, and anything answering on your public footprint that shouldn’t be.

✉

Email & Phishing Exposure

SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configuration, spoofability of your domain, and how exposed your team is to credential-phishing — the #1 small-business entry point.

🔑

Access & MFA Coverage

Who has access to what, stale accounts that should be gone, privilege sprawl, and where multi-factor authentication is missing or misconfigured.

🛠

Patch & Update Posture

Operating systems, firmware, plugins, and business applications — what’s behind on patches and which gaps are actively exploitable.

💾

Backup & Recovery Readiness

Whether your backups would actually survive a ransomware event: coverage, isolation, and whether restores have ever been tested.

📶

Network & Wi-Fi Hygiene

Segmentation, guest network isolation, default credentials on network gear, and remote-access paths into your environment.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need access to our systems to run the audit?
Yes, but it’s coordinated and read-only wherever possible. DPTC scopes access with you up front, works around your business hours, and does not disrupt production systems.
How long does a cybersecurity audit take?
Most small-business audits complete within one to two weeks depending on the number of systems, locations, and accounts in scope.
What do we actually receive?
A risk-ranked findings report, a plain-English executive summary for leadership, and a prioritized remediation roadmap — what to fix first, what it costs, and what can wait.
Will DPTC fix what the audit finds?
Yes. Remediation is quoted separately after the audit so you can fix issues in priority order — with DPTC, your own IT staff, or another vendor. The report is yours either way.
We’re a small company. Do we really need this?
Small businesses are targeted precisely because attackers assume weaker defenses and no dedicated security staff. An audit tells you exactly where you stand before an incident makes the decision for you.
